## Bottom line

> **No demonstrated S-PACE algorithmic advantage.**

This is the exact conclusion required by the frozen decision rule.  It is not
merely a conservative wording choice: both the Google primary gate and the
PNNL retention gate failed.

The result does show that the implementation is reproducible, that the
proper-prior randomization construction showed no detected calibration
failure at the audited threshold, and that the spectral component can recover
a real Google event.  Those are useful findings, but they do not establish an
advantage for the preregistered S-PACE composite.

## What the result means scientifically

1. **The simple observable was stronger here.** Detector firing rate and
   diagonal likelihood aligned better with both the event and decoder
   mismatch labels than the frozen high-dimensional composite.
2. **The spectral idea is not falsified, but the proposed composite is not
   validated.** The spectral branch detected the event and ranked some
   mismatches; the fixed composite did not.  Selecting spectral alone after
   seeing this result would be exploratory, not a Run 6 success.
3. **Calibration and discriminative utility are separate problems.** The
   formal e-process behaved plausibly under randomization, yet its frozen
   score aggregation was not useful enough on the target endpoints.
4. **“Maximum class difference” needs the right accessible observable.**
   A richer contrast space cannot recover information absent from the
   syndrome sigma-field, and it can dilute a strong low-dimensional signal.
5. **The reproducibility machinery is a genuine contribution.** The
   no-go-aware protocol, correct formal time unit, access separation,
   randomization calibration, resource ledger, and fail-closed claim
   generation remain useful even though the algorithmic gate failed.

## Most defensible next experiment

The next study should not retune Run 6 and call the result confirmatory.
Instead, use these observations to preregister a new prospective benchmark:

1. compare DFR, diagonal likelihood, spectral-only contrast, same-feature
   logistic, and a new adaptive aggregator at identical shot and calibration
   budgets;
2. treat the spectral-only branch as a new primary candidate rather than
   selecting it post hoc;
3. replace a fixed sparse/spectral mixture with a predictable
   regret-controlled expert aggregator, while preserving e-validity;
4. collect synchronized calibration interventions and decoder likelihoods;
5. include equal-circuit controls so hardware drift is not confounded with
   QASM changes;
6. evaluate multiple independent events and report event-level uncertainty;
7. measure per-method latency, energy, and memory if a systems advantage is
   claimed; and
8. freeze the success rule before any new held outcome is opened.

The concrete research hypothesis is that **predictable spectral witness
adaptation may be useful only when aggregation does not suppress the
spectral branch and when the evaluation target contains genuinely
multivariate drift beyond firing rate**.  Run 6 motivates that hypothesis;
it does not establish it.
