# Run 5 prior-art and claim-boundary audit

**Audit date:** 2026-07-27

**Applies to:** Run 5, *What Syndrome Data Can and Cannot Detect:
Accessible-Observable Sequential Change Detection for Topological Quantum
Error Correction*

**Controlling protocol:**
[`run5_surface_code_drift_preregistered_plan.md`](run5_surface_code_drift_preregistered_plan.md)

**Bibliography:** [`../publication/run5/references.bib`](../publication/run5/references.bib)

## 1. Executive conclusion

Run 5 must not be presented as discovering local indistinguishability,
Wilson/logical loops, surface-code drift detection, covariance whitening,
CUSUM, e-detectors, classical shadows, decoder reweighting, or syndrome-based
noise estimation. All of those have substantial prior foundations.

The defensible Run 5 contribution is narrower:

1. construct controlled spatial, temporal, and logical drift examples with an
   explicit hierarchy of accessible observations;
2. attach an exact information-loss certificate to each example;
3. recover a symmetry/locality-constrained witness when the richer observation
   level contains the missing information;
4. place that predictable bounded witness inside an established anytime-valid
   sequential-testing construction; and
5. compare it reproducibly with one physically matched, predeclared logistic
   comparator and separately labeled model-aware/QEC decoder controls.

This is a potentially useful synthesis and experimental framework. It is not,
by itself, evidence of algorithmic advantage. The logistic comparator was
chosen after inspecting the independent offline audit but before the
sequential locked test; it is not claimed to be the strongest generic
baseline. A positive locked result supports only vAOC versus that named
comparator on the two declared tasks after the Hoeffding/Holm, ARL, and
physical-budget criteria pass—not Helstrom discrimination, a known-post
likelihood oracle, eSCD in general, or all surface-code drift problems.

## 2. Evidence vocabulary

The manuscript should keep these four evidence levels distinct.

| Level | Meaning in Run 5 | What it can support |
|---|---|---|
| Exact statement | A proposition proved from stated assumptions | Only the mathematical model and observation class in the proposition |
| Controlled-model evidence | Exact finite-mixture calculations or Monte Carlo samples from the declared phenomenological model | Reproducibility and performance inside that model |
| Circuit-level evidence | Stim stabilizer-circuit/DEM samples decoded with PyMatching | Robustness to that explicit simulated circuit and noise model |
| Hardware evidence | Samples generated by a physical QEC device | **Not produced by Run 5 unless a separately documented hardware arm is added** |

“Exact” must not be used as a synonym for “physically complete.” A finite
controlled detector model may have an exact likelihood while omitting leakage,
non-Pauli noise, calibration drift, coherent faults, scheduling effects, and
other device mechanisms.

## 3. Prior art that must be credited

### 3.1 Topological codes, local blindness, and logical loops

- Kitaev introduced the anyonic/topological-code framework underlying the
  toric code [kitaev2003]; Dennis, Kitaev, Landahl, and Preskill developed the
  topological-memory and decoding picture relevant to surface codes
  [dennis2002].
- The Knill--Laflamme conditions already characterize when errors are
  indistinguishable on a code space [knill1997]. Bravyi, Hastings, and
  Michalakis formalized local topological-order conditions and stability under
  local perturbations [bravyi2010].
- A closed error chain has zero syndrome boundary, while a homologically
  nontrivial cycle may act logically. Run 5's loop example is an executable
  no-go certificate built from this established stabilizer/topological-code
  structure, not a new toric-code theorem.
- Gauge-region observables require care because the local gauge-invariant
  algebra can have a center and need not induce a naive tensor-product
  factorization [casini2014]. Ribbon-operator recovery is also established as a
  route for detecting topological order [bridgeman2016].

Primary sources:
[Kitaev](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9707021),
[Dennis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0110143),
[Bravyi--Hastings--Michalakis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0344),
[Casini--Huerta--Rosabal](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.1183), and
[Bridgeman--Flammia--Poulin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02275).

### 3.2 Optimal and restricted discrimination

- Helstrom's binary quantum decision theory supplies the optimal unrestricted
  one-shot discrimination benchmark for specified states and priors
  [helstrom1976]. A Run 5 method cannot outperform that benchmark on the same
  state ensemble, measurement resources, loss, and prior.
- Once measurements are restricted, the relevant distinguishability is the
  distinguishability norm induced by the allowed measurement family, not the
  unrestricted trace norm [matthews2009]. Group symmetry in quantum hypothesis
  testing is also established [hiai2009].
- A positive-eigenspace projector of a state difference coincides with the
  Helstrom binary effect only when the same two states, priors/costs, and
  unrestricted binary-effect class are being optimized. A
  symmetry/locality-constrained AOC witness, a learned finite-calibration
  witness, and a sequential delay objective are different problems.
- Run 5 normally receives an already classical syndrome stream. Helstrom is
  therefore a conceptual ceiling for an upstream quantum-measurement problem,
  not a directly implemented baseline for every syndrome experiment.

Primary sources:
[Matthews--Wehner--Winter](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-009-0890-5) and
[Hiai--Mosonyi--Hayashi](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.0704).

### 3.3 Classical sequential detection, whitening, and growth objectives

- Hotelling's multivariate statistic is the classical foundation for
  covariance-whitened mean contrasts [hotelling1931]. Calling
  \(V^{-1}\delta\) a new discriminative direction is not justified.
- In unrestricted quantum-state tangent space, the analogous
  variance-sensitive local geometry is tied to the established symmetric
  logarithmic derivative and quantum Fisher information
  [braunstein1994]. Run 5 does not introduce that geometry.
- Page's cumulative-sum construction is foundational sequential
  change-detection prior art [page1954].
- Kelly's expected-log-growth criterion explains why a likelihood ratio or
  well-specified betting factor targets multiplicative evidence growth rather
  than a raw expectation gap [kelly1956].
- Shin, Ramdas, and Rinaldo introduced e-detectors and proved nonasymptotic
  average-run-length control for composite/nonparametric sequential change
  detection [shin2024]. Run 5's restarted/mixture e-process construction must
  be presented as an application or specialization of this framework.

Primary sources:
[Hotelling](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-mathematical-statistics/volume-2/issue-3/The-Generalization-of-Students-Ratio/10.1214/aoms/1177732979.short),
[Page](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100),
[Kelly](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1956.tb03809.x), and
[e-detectors](https://nejsds.nestat.org/journal/NEJSDS/article/59/text);
for quantum statistical geometry, see
[Braunstein--Caves](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.72.3439).

### 3.4 Classical shadows and quantum sequential change detection

- Classical shadows provide a randomized-measurement estimator for many
  observables from reusable snapshots [huang2020].
- Zecchin, Simeone, and Ramdas combine classical shadows with e-detectors in
  eSCD, with average-run-length control and finite-sample delay guarantees for
  observable-defined quantum changes [zecchin2026].
- A local-Clifford shadow measurement audit in Run 5 is not an implementation
  of the full eSCD protocol unless it implements eSCD's measurement,
  observable, e-detector, stopping, and guarantee assumptions.
- Native all-\(Z\) syndrome readout is task-matched for diagonal \(Z\)-type
  observables. If it needs fewer copies than a universal shadow scheme for
  those observables, that demonstrates the cost of measurement universality;
  it does not show that the Run 5 algorithm dominates eSCD.

Primary sources:
[classical shadows](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-020-0932-7) and
[eSCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11846).

### 3.5 Syndrome-based drift/noise learning and decoder adaptation

- DGR uses decoded matching-edge and edge-pair statistics to adapt decoding
  graph weights under drift and correlated noise [wang2023dgr]. It directly
  prevents a claim that Run 5 is the first syndrome-driven drift-aware
  reweighting method.
- Blume-Kohout and Young estimate probabilities of detector-error-model events
  and event classes from repeated syndrome extraction [blumekohout2025].
- Takou, Benito, Vezvaee, Lidar, and Brown estimate DEM event probabilities
  from surface-code experiment syndromes and use them as decoder/logical-error
  priors [takou2026].
- Kobori and Todo infer general noise-model parameters from surface-code
  syndrome statistics using tensor-network simulation and Bayesian methods;
  their time-varying arm uses sequential Monte Carlo [kobori2025].

This prior art means that “uses syndrome correlations,” “learns noise from
syndromes,” “tracks time-varying noise,” and “adapts a decoder” are not
novelty claims. Run 5 may instead compare information restrictions and ask
whether its constrained witness is useful under a fixed calibration/shots
budget.

Metadata warning: arXiv:2606.11496 is titled **“Logical error estimation from
syndrome data of surface-code experiments,”** not “Estimating detector error
models from syndrome data.” The latter is the distinct arXiv:2504.14643
preprint.

Primary sources:
[DGR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16214),
[DEM estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14643),
[logical-error estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11496), and
[Bayesian inference](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/wg5h-spy6).

### 3.6 Simulation and decoding software

- Stim is an established high-performance stabilizer circuit simulator
  [gidney2021].
- PyMatching is an established minimum-weight perfect-matching decoder
  [higgott2022], and Sparse Blossom is its later sparse matching algorithm
  [higgott2025].

Using these packages provides a reproducible circuit-level validation route.
It is not a new decoder, a hardware implementation, or evidence of quantum
speedup.

Primary sources:
[Stim](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2021-07-06-497/),
[PyMatching](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3505637), and
[Sparse Blossom](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2025-01-20-1600/).

## 4. Exact Run 5 statements and their limits

This section distinguishes a correct theorem from a broad novelty claim. The
first and fourth statements below are standard consequences of probability
and sequential-testing theory. The loop statement is established QEC
structure. The controlled spatial/temporal constructions may be original
repository contributions, but this audit does not establish literature-wide
priority and the paper should not use “first” language.

### 4.1 Accessible-process no-go certificate

Let \(Y=(Y_1,Y_2,\ldots)\) be the full stream and let
\(Z=\mathcal H(Y)\) be the complete stream accessible to a detector. The map
\(\mathcal H\) may include per-round compression, a finite history, or an
audit policy. Let \(P_0^Z=\mathcal H_\#P_0\) and
\(P_1^Z=\mathcal H_\#P_1\) be probability measures on the **entire accessible
path**.

**Proposition (accessible-process no-go).** If
\[
P_0^Z=P_1^Z,
\]
then every randomized stopping rule whose randomization is independent of the
data and whose decisions are measurable with respect to the accessible
filtration has the same stopping-time distribution under \(P_0\) and \(P_1\).
Consequently, at every horizon \(T\),
\[
P_1(\tau\leq T)=P_0(\tau\leq T).
\]

**Proof.** Include the detector's independent random seed \(U\) in the
accessible space. The stopping time is a measurable function
\(\tau=\phi(Z,U)\). Equality of the input measures
\(P_0^Z\otimes P^U=P_1^Z\otimes P^U\) implies equality of every measurable
pushforward, including the law of \(\tau\). \(\square\)

**Necessary correction to shorthand statements.** Equality of only the
one-round pushforwards,
\[
h_\#P_0(Y_t)=h_\#P_1(Y_t),
\]
does **not** by itself imply the sequential no-go when temporal dependence can
change. The theorem requires equality of the accessible process law. In the
Run 5 spatial arm, identical one-round accessible laws imply identical path
laws only because the declared rounds are independent/product distributed. In
the temporal arm, the one-round syndrome law is deliberately identical while
the two-round/path law differs; a history-aware detector can therefore have
power.

This correction should appear in the paper, tests, and captions wherever the
no-go result is summarized.

### 4.2 Spatial controlled-model certificate and witness recovery

Within the declared periodic finite-mixture/BSC model, Run 5 can prove:

1. the complete detector-count distribution is independent of the chain-length
   mixture parameter \(q\), not merely that its mean or variance is unchanged;
2. independent rounds therefore induce identical count-process laws for
   different \(q\), giving an exact count-only no-go certificate;
3. the uncompressed syndrome likelihood depends on the declared translation
   statistics \((\bar z,g_1,g_2)\); and
4. the population pair-feature contrast has the stated signed
   displacement pattern.

These are exact statements about the controlled family. They do not imply that
those statistics are sufficient for an arbitrary surface-code circuit, an
arbitrary correlated DEM, or hardware noise.

### 4.3 Temporal controlled-model separation

Within the declared stationary two-state latent construction, Run 5 can prove:

1. the complete one-cycle syndrome distribution is the same before and after
   the change;
2. the adjacent-pair distribution changes when the persistence parameter
   changes; and
3. the stated adjacent-pair likelihood ratio has null expectation one.

The nonoverlapping-pair detector is a pair-restricted detector. It is not the
full likelihood for the correlated alternative. A forward-filter HMM
likelihood may be used as a model-aware ceiling, with the changepoint-candidate
resolution stated exactly.

### 4.4 Logical-cycle pathwise certificate

For a chain complex with boundary map \(\partial\), a closed chain
\(c\in\ker\partial\) satisfies
\[
\partial(E+c)=\partial E.
\]
Thus a coupled error path and the same path plus a closed logical cycle have
identical syndromes. If the added cycle is homologically nontrivial, it can
change a logical/Wilson observable while remaining syndrome-invisible.

This is a useful pathwise certificate and negative control. It is not a new
Wilson-loop, toric-code, local-topological-order, string-theory, or holographic
result.

### 4.5 Predictable bounded e-factor

Suppose \(s_t\in[-1,1]\) is measurable after observing time \(t\), its witness
and scaling are predictable from \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\), and the exact null
satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_0[s_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=0.
\]
For a predictable \(\beta_t\in[0,1]\),
\[
L_t=1+\beta_t s_t\geq0,\qquad
\mathbb E_0[L_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=1.
\]
Restarted sums or mixtures can then be placed in the established e-detector
framework. The resulting average-run-length statement comes from that
framework, not from a new Run 5 sequential-testing theorem.

If the witness/tuning rule uses the current or future evaluation sample, or
if centering or the declared score bound is invalid, the displayed
conditional identity need not hold. Run 5 instead uses an analytic null mean.
Conditional on independent auxiliary data, covariance, ridge, and
logistic-direction fitting only select a fixed or predictable bounded effect;
centering at the analytic null therefore preserves the exact identity and ARL
argument. A variant that estimates the null mean from finite data without
uncertainty protection does not automatically inherit that validity and must
use a separate uncertainty-valid proof or be labeled empirically calibrated.

## 5. What controlled evidence can establish

The following language is valid only after the corresponding checks pass.
This audit intentionally contains no experimental result values.

### 5.1 Exact controlled arm

The exact arm can establish that:

- the declared compressed observation is provably blind in the declared model;
- a richer, still accessible correlation observable contains the missing
  information;
- likelihood normalization, sufficiency, score boundedness, and null
  e-factor identities agree with analytic calculations; and
- matched simulated streams permit paired comparison of delay,
  false-alarm behavior, runtime, and memory.

It cannot establish device realism, universal sample efficiency, or asymptotic
computational advantage.

### 5.2 Circuit-level arm

A Stim/PyMatching arm can establish that a result persists for the exact
reported stabilizer circuit, DEM conversion, injected noise/drift, decoder,
distance, rounds, package versions, shots, and seeds. It remains simulated
circuit-level evidence. A generated Stim circuit is not hardware data and is
not automatically a production-complete model of a named device.

### 5.3 Shadow measurement-policy audit

A correct local-Clifford shadow estimator can quantify copy cost for estimating
the chosen observables and compare it with native all-\(Z\) measurement. This
is a measurement-policy audit. It cannot by itself validate eSCD, demonstrate
a quantum advantage, or support a claim that universal shadows are inferior
for settings in which the future observable is not known to the measurement
device.

### 5.4 Decoder utility

A practical decoder-benefit statement requires decoded logical outcomes, not
just earlier alarms or better noise-parameter estimates. It also requires:

- identical data/calibration budgets;
- a declared delay between alarm, model estimation, and decoder update;
- comparison with a static decoder and strong syndrome-adaptive baselines;
- separation of detector-estimation data from evaluation outcomes; and
- uncertainty on logical-error differences.

Without this arm, Run 5 is a drift-detection study with a QEC-motivated data
model, not evidence of improved fault-tolerant computation.

## 6. Named-comparator decision and permitted language

### 6.1 What would support the named Run 5 comparison

The preregistered comparison asks whether vAOC has a lower
restart-at-change restricted mean detection delay than the named,
predeclared logistic comparator, with:

- both Bonferroni-Hoeffding upper bounds below zero and both Holm-adjusted
  one-sided Hoeffding p-values below 0.05;
- the required false-alarm/average-run-length condition met;
- identical locked-test streams and matched \(8\times512\)-cycle auxiliary
  stream structures/budgets;
- no validation-to-test leakage; and
- the locked design run at publication-grade scale rather than reported as a
  pilot.

If satisfied, an accurate claim is:

> In the two preregistered controlled tasks and at the declared physical
> budgets, the
> variance-aware accessible-observable detector reduced the specified delay
> metric relative to the named predeclared logistic comparator while meeting the
> stated false-alarm criterion.

This does not establish a strongest-baseline or general algorithmic advantage.

The exact likelihood, known-post CUSUM, full-HMM oracle, and Helstrom bound are
ceilings or conceptual limits; Run 5 is not expected or permitted to claim
superiority over them. The same applies to a separately costed direct
Wilson/logical-observable oracle. If the discriminative information is already
a known scalar parity feature, a correctly specified threshold or logistic
model on that identical feature is also a mandatory simple comparator; merely
re-expressing the feature through AOC/ECA is not an advantage.

### 6.2 Claim-language table

| Status | Language |
|---|---|
| Permitted after analytic verification | “We construct an exact controlled drift whose complete count-process law is invariant, while signed spatial correlations change.” |
| Permitted after analytic verification | “We give an executable accessible-process no-go certificate and recover a witness at the next observation level.” |
| Permitted under exact-null assumptions | “The predictable bounded score instantiates an e-detector with the stated average-run-length guarantee.” |
| Permitted after a locked positive result | “vAOC reduced the declared delay relative to the named predeclared logistic comparator on both controlled tasks under the stated physical budgets and ARL condition.” |
| Permitted after circuit simulation | “The effect persists in the reported Stim/PyMatching circuit-level simulations.” |
| Conditional on logical-outcome evidence | “The alarm-and-update pipeline reduces decoded logical error relative to the named decoder baselines.” |
| Prohibited | “The method beats Helstrom,” “beats the oracle,” or “is universally optimal.” |
| Prohibited | Superiority to a direct Wilson/logical audit, or to a threshold/logistic rule supplied with the identical known parity feature, unless an explicitly matched experiment actually establishes that narrower comparison. |
| Prohibited | “Quantum speedup,” “universal sample-efficiency advantage,” or “scalable computational advantage,” absent a separate complexity/resource theorem and matching evidence. |
| Prohibited | “First QEC drift detector,” “first syndrome noise estimator,” “first correlation-aware decoder,” or “first covariance/e-process detector.” |
| Prohibited | “We discover Wilson loops/local indistinguishability/topological order.” |
| Prohibited | “We solve string theory,” “demonstrate holographic duality,” or infer either from the surface-code examples. |

### 6.3 Null or mixed results remain informative

If the locked detector does not beat the frozen baseline, Run 5 can still
publish:

- exact negative certificates showing when a compressed statistic is blind;
- a reproducible benchmark that separates one-cycle and history information;
- a measurement-universality cost audit;
- failure modes of constrained witnesses relative to likelihood and generic
  covariance methods; and
- evidence about whether detector gains do or do not translate to decoder
  gains.

These are stronger and more credible than retroactively changing the advantage
definition.

## 7. Untested claims as of this audit

Unless later evidence is added and separately audited, Run 5 does not test:

- physical quantum hardware;
- non-Pauli/coherent/leakage noise in the exact controlled arm;
- open-system or non-Markovian device physics beyond the declared latent model;
- universal robustness across code families, decoders, distances, or
  measurement schedules;
- superiority to DGR, Bayesian sequential noise inference, syndrome-to-DEM
  estimation, or eSCD under faithful implementations and matched resources;
- superiority to an oracle Wilson/logical audit or a threshold/logistic rule
  given the same already identified parity feature;
- finite-calibration anytime validity;
- asymptotic time, memory, communication, energy, or sample-complexity
  advantage;
- a quantum computational or sensing advantage;
- a new QEC/topological-order theorem; or
- any string-theory or holographic-duality claim.

Future gauge/string applications may compare gauge-invariant reduced
observable algebras and resolve charge/flux sectors in lattice gauge models or
tensor networks. That is a concrete research direction, not evidence supplied
by Run 5.

## 9. Manuscript audit checklist

Before a Run 5 PDF is released:

- [ ] Replace every one-round-only no-go formulation with the
      accessible-**process** formulation, adding the iid/product corollary
      where applicable.
- [ ] State whether each reported dataset is exact controlled simulation,
      circuit-level stabilizer simulation, or hardware data.
- [ ] Cite e-detectors for the ARL construction and separate exact-null from
      finite-calibration claims.
- [ ] Cite Hotelling/Fisher-style whitening, Page/CUSUM, and Kelly/log-growth
      rather than presenting them as ECA/AOC inventions.
- [ ] Cite eSCD and classical shadows; call the local-Clifford experiment a
      measurement-policy audit unless the full protocol is implemented.
- [ ] Cite DGR, both syndrome-to-DEM papers, and Bayesian syndrome-noise
      inference before making any QEC adaptation claim.
- [ ] Report Stim/PyMatching versions and call their output circuit-level
      simulation.
- [ ] Use the exact title of arXiv:2606.11496 and preserve preprint statuses.
- [ ] State the frozen comparison set and advantage criterion before giving a
      result conclusion.
- [ ] Include explicit limitations covering oracles, Helstrom, quantum
      advantage, scalability, topology, and string/holography.
